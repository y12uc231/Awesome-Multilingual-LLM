## Tokenization & Vocabulary

- `2026-11` **[MoTo: Mixture of Tokenizers Towards Fair Multilingual Language Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11027)** — Altintas & Raffel · *NeurIPS 2026*<br>MoTo: composes per-language BPE tokenizers into a deduplicated superset vocabulary, keeping English performance while improving 18 of 21 languages across six scripts.
- `2026-10` **[Language-Specific Effects of Tokenizer Choice in Multilingual Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12144)** — Meister et al.<br>Trains 123 models with 54 tokenizers, finding tokenizer choice matters more for languages with less training data and leaving a language out of tokenizer training raises its bits-per-byte.
- `2026-09` **[To Each Language Its Tokenizer: Modular Tokenizers for Efficient Multilingual LLMs](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.1019/)** — Talla et al. · *Findings of EMNLP 2026* · [[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15528)]<br>Modular BPE and Unigram tokenizers yield language-subset subtokenizers matching monolingual compression and improving cross-lingual fairness, with pretraining that reduces memory and speeds inference.
- `2026-07` **[The Tokenizer Tax: Quantifying and Explaining the Cross-Lingual Cost of Subword Tokenization for Indian Languages](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24276)** — Srivastava<br>Measures fertility of six tokenizers for 14 Indian languages on FLORES-200; cl100k_base imposes an average 8.0x tax relative to English, 13.0x for Malayalam, driven by failed byte-pair merges.
- `2026-07` **[Language Models are not Equally Robust to Non-Canonical Tokenization across Languages](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26831)** — Ghosh & Jyothi<br>Shows tokenization invariance does not generalize across 27 languages: instruction-tuned models drop 23.7% (Llama-3.1-8B), 11.4% (Qwen3-8B) and 9.9% (Gemma-3-12B) on average, more for fragmented languages.
- `2026-07` **[In-Place Tokenizer Expansion for Pre-trained LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15232)** — Smith et al.<br>Continues existing BPE merges on a multilingual corpus to expand pre-trained tokenizers in place; on LFM2-8B-A1B it encodes Hindi and Vietnamese in about 2.4x and 2.6x fewer tokens.
- `2026-06` **[Phonemes to the Rescue: Multilingual Tokenization Based on International Phonetic Alphabet](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1872/)** — Miletić et al. · *ACL 2026* · [[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20993)]<br>Trains matched text and International Phonetic Alphabet subword tokenizers across 24 languages and 14 scripts; IPA tokenizers improve tokenization quality, especially for non-Latin scripts, and generalize to unseen languages.
- `2026-06` **[LangMAP: A Language-Adaptive Approach to Tokenization](https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.2170/)** — Meister et al. · *EMNLP 2026* · [[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23566)]<br>LangMAP: extends UnigramLM with one distribution per language over a shared vocabulary, raising morphological boundary recall, though fine-tuning shows no significant gains for most language-task pairs.
- `2026-06` **[Equity with Efficiency: An Empirical Study of Tokenizers for Multilingual Large Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.1783/)** — Lee et al. · *EMNLP 2026* · [[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15044)]<br>Compares equitable tokenizers on 11 Southeast Asian languages with controlled 1.5B-parameter model training; Parity-aware BPE lies on the efficiency-equity Pareto frontier.
- `2026-04` **[MIXAR: Scaling Autoregressive Pixel-based Language Models to Multiple Languages and Scripts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11575)** — Hu et al.<br>MIXAR: generative multilingual pixel-based language model up to 0.5B parameters on eight languages, generalizing to unseen low-resource languages better than a tokenizer-based counterpart, with GRPO-based training.
- `2026-03` **[What Really Controls Temporal Reasoning in Large Language Models: Tokenisation or Representation of Time?](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.2149/)** — Bhatia et al. · *Findings of EMNLP 2026* · [[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19017)]<br>MultiTempBench: 15,000 temporal reasoning examples in five languages and three calendars; across 20 LLMs, tokenisation fragmentation of dates collapses accuracy in low-resource languages.
- `2026-03` **[Separate Before You Compress: The WWHO Tokenization Architecture](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25309)** — Darshana<br>WWHO/SGPE: syllable-aware grapheme pair encoding separating script rules from compression for Abugida scripts, cutting tokens 61.7% (Sinhala) and 27.0% (Hindi) versus o200k.
- `2026-01` **[TReX: Tokenizer Regression for Optimal Data Mixture](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.298/)** — Won et al. · *EACL 2026* · [[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13588)]<br>TREX: regression on small proxy tokenizers predicts the best language mixture for multilingual tokenizer training, beating LLaMA3-based and uniform mixtures by up to 12% in compression.
- `2026` **[When Tokenizers Fail: Byte-Level Chunking for Zero-Shot Transfer to Low-Resource Languages](https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.2139/)** — Kumar et al. · *EMNLP 2026* · [[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27658)]<br>Initializes byte embeddings from a frozen model's subword representations and aligns byte chunks to subword targets with POS supervision, improving POS tagging up to 13.3% across six languages.
